Track 3 · methodological comparison

Interconnection Queues vs. Satellite / Permit Mapping

What public electrical records reveal that physical mapping does not, what physical mapping reveals that queues do not, and why their disagreement is itself a useful research object.

← Main registryPlan A bridgeAI data-center explorer
Scope boundary. The interconnection core is North America-only public-evidence coverage. Its strongest direct use is the Phase 1-style question of what large compute/load is publicly evidenced and who is associated with it; it is not a bilateral U.S.–China verification system. The public-source boundary also means a genuinely covert project could avoid these signals. The defensible capability claim is to raise the cost of hiding and map the ambiguous middle, not to claim discovery of every secret facility.

1. Two different observation layers

Interconnection / connection records

A queue or connection record is an electrical-system observation. Depending on the market, public records can expose requested capacity, project/request identity, point of interconnection, study status, dates, developer/applicant fields, and transmission or distribution relationships.

The timing advantage is that an interconnection request can exist before physical construction is visible. It can therefore reveal large-load intent while a project is still a planning or study object.

The hard boundary is equally important: it only sees projects that enter the public connection process represented by the source. It can miss compute built on existing capacity, behind-the-meter or privately supplied generation, existing service arrangements, unclear customers, cancelled/renamed projects, and jurisdictions without useful public queue data.

Satellite imagery / permits / physical mapping

Satellite and permit evidence is a physical-construction observation. It can reveal land clearing, building shells, roof completion, substations, cooling equipment, turbines, batteries, roads, construction sequencing and other visible infrastructure, while permits can add building, mechanical and electrical documentation.

Its advantage is physical realization: it can show that something was actually built rather than merely requested. Its blind spot is that intent can precede visible construction, and physical evidence alone may not identify the exact tenant, current hardware population, or workload.

2. The timing distinction

QUEUE / CONNECTION

project request → study / approval → construction may follow

SATELLITE / PERMIT

land / construction / equipment → commissioning → physical operation becomes observable

The intervals are project- and market-specific. An interconnection record can therefore provide an earlier signal than imagery for some projects, while imagery can provide a later physical confirmation of what the electrical record represented.

Neither layer should be collapsed into a single “facility found” field.

3. The overlap is more informative than either list alone

The repository preserves the September 2026 Epoch AI snapshot as a separate evidence layer: 93 sites, with 545 dated timeline observations and 227 chip-quantity observations. The current build also maintains a conservative connection crosswalk for all 93 Epoch sites.

RelationshipInterpretation
Queue evidence + physical evidenceElectrical intent and physical realization point toward the same project/site; identity and capacity still require careful matching.
Queue evidence without physical evidenceCould indicate an earlier-stage, delayed, cancelled, non-public physical realization, or an identity/geography problem.
Physical evidence without queue evidenceCould indicate existing-capacity expansion, another connection pathway, private generation, insufficient queue coverage, or unresolved identity.
NeitherNo public signal in these layers, not evidence that no project or compute exists.

4. Why the Epoch cross-reference matters

Epoch's current public AI-data-center dataset describes independent estimates based on high-resolution satellite imagery, permits and public documents, with site timelines and estimates of IT power, compute and cost. The registry starts from a different observation stream: public electrical-system records. It tries to resolve project/entity/site relationships without treating queue MW and AI-data-center IT MW as identical quantities.

Where do the two methods agree, where do they disagree, and what does each disagreement tell us to investigate next?

5. Relation to Naci Cankaya's work

Cankaya's 2024 BlueDot project, Are you secretly training AI?, explicitly explored detecting covert AI training through physical signatures including power consumption, cooling requirements and computational hardware characteristics. The project describes itself as a theoretical exploration and a four-week project rather than an empirical dataset.

The registry is a different kind of contribution: it applies the grid-interconnection slice of that physical-signature idea at nine real North American organized-market public systems, with source preservation, entity resolution, dated captures and an explicit ambiguous tier.

You explored a detection category from unavoidable physical signatures; I built a public-data implementation of the grid-interconnection slice of that idea at nine-market scale.

6. Relation to AI 2040 / Plan A

This registry is best treated as a supporting observability layer, not as the international verification regime itself. Its strongest direct contribution is the Phase 1-style accounting question:

What large compute/load is publicly evidenced, and who is associated with it?

It does not by itself provide bilateral U.S.–China coverage, treaty-grade inspection, hardware attestation, workload verification, or a method for discovering facilities intentionally designed to leave no public signal.

7. The two hard limits

Geographic limit

The main interconnection registry is North American. It therefore cannot by itself provide the bilateral U.S.–China visibility that a mature international Track 3 verification system would require.

Disclosure limit

The registry is built from public evidence. A genuinely covert project would have an incentive to avoid public queue, permit, utility and similar disclosure signals.

Absence of a public signal is an unresolved evidence state, not evidence of absence.

8. Research implication

The high-value next step is not simply adding more facilities. It is systematically measuring the disagreement set between electrical, physical, and other independent signals: queue first, physical evidence later, reconcile identity and capacity, retain divergence as a research target, add independent evidence where available, and never silently promote unresolved cases.
